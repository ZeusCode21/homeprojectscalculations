1 · Lead sources

Each channel's cost per sign-up, sign-ups per $100, cost per paying user and return on each dollar. Set a target cost per sign-up and the last column shows what has to change to hit it. Spend per month is set in section 3.

Sign-ups here are before referrals. Cost per paying user = cost per sign-up ÷ the share of sign-ups who buy a plan (section 2). Return = what a sign-up is worth ÷ what it costs. 3× or more is healthy; under 1× loses money on every sign-up.

2 · Plans and what a sign-up is worth

Conversion = the share of new sign-ups who buy that plan. Lifetime value per buyer = what you keep per sale × how long they stay (monthly) or how many times they buy (one-time). Value per sign-up adds it all up, because most sign-ups never pay.

How it works

Sign-ups each month come from every lead source that's on and past its start month, × (1 + referrals per user). Active users = last month's minus the ones who finish (1 ÷ months a user stays) plus new sign-ups.

Lead source types: Views (your reels) = views ÷ 1,000 × sign-ups per 1,000 views, with views growing to a cap. Search (SEO) = visits × visit-to-sign-up %. Creators = creators × views ÷ 1,000 × sign-ups per 1,000; cost = creators × base pay + views ÷ 1,000 × view bonus. Impressions (Meta, TikTok) = spend ÷ CPM × 1,000 × click-through × landing sign-up. Clicks (Google) = spend ÷ CPC × click-to-sign-up. Pay per sign-up = spend ÷ price per sign-up. Fixed (in person, email) = a set number of sign-ups each month.

Plans: buyers = new sign-ups × conversion. In a plan's launch month it can also sell to everyone already active (setting in section 4). Monthly subscribers stay until they cancel; average stay = 1 ÷ monthly churn. You keep = price − payment fees (web or App Store) − renders × render price − other cost per sale.

Value per sign-up = Σ (conversion × lifetime value per buyer) + materials commissions per user − what a free user costs you over their stay. Value per paying user = plan value per sign-up ÷ share who pay. Return = value per sign-up ÷ cost per sign-up (the same as payer value ÷ cost per payer).

What it takes: cost per sign-up moves in proportion to the main rate of each source (landing sign-up %, click-to-sign-up %, sign-ups per 1,000 views), so the needed rate = today's rate × today's cost ÷ target cost.

What I changed from Grok's assessment

  • 10% of sign-ups pay → about 2 to 3%. Freemium apps convert a median 2.1% of downloads to paid in 35 days (2.6% in North America). AI apps convert 2.4% (RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026). 10% is what hard-paywall apps get (10.7%). Set that if you put the design tool behind a paywall from the first render.
  • 10% monthly churn → 20%. Monthly plans keep only about 7 to 9% of subscribers after a year, and AI apps' monthly plans retain 36% worse than other apps (RevenueCat 2026). Projects also end, so people cancel when the deck is built.
  • LTV of $80 per Design payer → about $40. Same $9.99 to $14.99 price, but at 20% churn and after renders and fees. With CAC per payer at 2% conversion, UGC is close to break-even, not 8:1.
  • Google ads cost more here. Home & Home Improvement clicks average $7.85 (WordStream 2025), among the highest of any industry.

Kept from Grok: your UGC deal ($500 base, $1.50 per 1,000 views, 2 sign-ups per 1,000 views), the plan structure (small-item pack, Design Plus, Build Project Pass, Pro), the render guardrails, and contractor leads later at $15 to $60.
